# Category Playbooks

How each business category adapts the format library. Read the section matching the user's business; it lists the priority formats, the category's rules, and what to avoid.

## D2C physical products

**Priority formats:** problem-solution (1), before/after (2), testimonial (3), benefit stack (5), offer stack (6), listicle (9), product hero (14).
**Rules:** cold traffic is problem-aware first, product second: name the pain before the product appears. The product must render accurately in every ad; it is the trust anchor. Faces and hands raise attention when the format allows a human element.
**Avoid:** feature jargon; a D2C static sells the felt outcome, not the spec sheet.
**Category notes:** beauty and wellness lean on before/after within claim limits; food on appetite-led product hero and offer stacks; apparel on lifestyle-context problem-solution and hero shots; home on before/after room contexts.

## SaaS

**Priority formats:** screenshot hero (11), metric-first (10), testimonial (3), us-vs-the-old-way (4), feature callout (12) applied to the UI, listicle (9) for use cases.
**Rules:** proof of capability beats authenticity theater: show the product doing the thing. The headline and the visible UI must describe the same outcome. Quantified claims ("saves the 4-hour reporting task") outperform identity claims ("feel like a better founder"). Use-case specificity: name the role and the workflow in the headline where possible.
**Avoid:** lifestyle imagery without product proof, vague benefit lines ("work smarter"), UGC-style gushing, urgency mechanics (multi-stakeholder purchases resist "ends tonight").
**Asset note:** the "product" is a UI screenshot. Have the user supply a clean, legible screenshot of the exact feature the claim references. Run one test render first; Shhots is optimized for physical products, so verify the screenshot survives composition before batching.

## Mobile apps

**Priority formats:** screenshot hero (11) in a phone frame, metric-first (10), testimonial (3), before/after (2) as with-app vs without-app moments, listicle (9).
**Rules:** the app screen is the product; keep the key screen legible at feed size. One screen per ad. Store-badge CTAs only as the user's official assets in post.
**Avoid:** cramming a multi-screen journey into one static.

## Service businesses (agencies, professionals, B2B services)

**Priority formats:** testimonial (3), metric-first (10) with a real client result, founder POV (7), us-vs-the-old-way (4), advertorial (8), listicle (9) for process steps.
**Rules:** the "product" is the outcome and the people; the visual anchor is the founder, the team, the deliverable, or the result artifact (a report, a build, a transformation photo). Specific beats general: "for [niche] businesses" outperforms "for everyone".
**Avoid:** stock-photo handshake energy; if the scene could belong to any company, it sells none.

## Local businesses

**Priority formats:** offer stack (6), testimonial (3), before/after (2) for trades and salons, product hero (14) for food, founder POV (7).
**Rules:** name the place: the neighborhood or city in the headline is the targeting made visible. Real interior/product photos as the base beat generic scenes.
**Avoid:** claims that outrun what a walk-in can verify.

## Funnel map recap (all categories)

Cold: formats 1, 2, 8, 9, 10. Warm: 3, 4, 7, 12, 13. Retargeting: 5, 6, plus an urgency line on any prior winner. Test format vs format first, then headlines inside the winner, then visual treatment.
