# Style Recipes

One section per style. Each contains: aesthetic (in renderable terms), treatment template for `shhots_create_product_video`, script notes, watch-outs. Replace bracketed slots. In every style, the product itself is rendered faithfully: correct label, shape, colors.

## 1. Claymation

**Aesthetic:** handcrafted clay world: visible fingerprints in surfaces, slightly imperfect edges, 12-frames-per-second stepped motion feel, miniature-set depth of field.

**Treatment:** Setting: a miniature handcrafted clay [environment matching product context], visible sculpting texture on every surface, tiny prop details. Camera: locked-off tabletop framing with occasional slow push-ins, macro depth of field. Lighting: soft warm practical light like a miniature film set. Actions: clay characters [discover / pass along / react to] the real product, which appears photorealistic and accurate against the clay world; character movement has a stepped, stop-motion cadence. Pacing: gentle, whimsical beats, one comedic pause. Product moments: the real product held or presented by clay hands at [mid-point], label readable. CTA: clay characters arrange around the product for the closing frame with CTA text.

**Script notes:** third-person narrator voiceover or short character lines; humor from the contrast between the handmade world and the real product.

**Watch-outs:** keep the clay style on the environment and characters only, never the product.

## 2. Animated (motion design)

**Aesthetic:** flat or semi-flat graphic scenes in brand colors, kinetic typography, shape transitions.

**Treatment:** Setting: stylized graphic environment in [brand palette]. Camera: simulated pans and zooms between scenes, transitions driven by shapes wiping the frame. Lighting: flat graphic contrast. Actions: animated elements illustrate [use case or mechanism] while the accurately rendered product anchors each scene. Pacing: beat-synced, energetic, a scene change every 2-3 seconds. Product moments: product present in every scene, hero-scaled in the final third. CTA: animated end card with CTA text.

**Script notes:** voiceover carries the argument; visuals illustrate rather than repeat it.

**Watch-outs:** motion design ages fast; anchor the look to the brand palette, not trends.

## 3. Podcast clip

**Aesthetic:** the familiar podcast-clip crop: speaker at a microphone, dark studio, big animated captions, waveform accents.

**Treatment:** Setting: podcast studio corner, large microphone in frame, dark backdrop with soft accent lighting, headphones visible. Camera: static medium close-up on the presenter, occasional slow zoom on emphasis lines. Lighting: moody key light with a colored rim. Actions: presenter speaks conversationally as if answering a host, gestures naturally, picks up the product at the key claim and keeps it in frame. Pacing: continuous take feel with caption emphasis carrying the rhythm. Product moments: product on the desk from the start, in hand from [7s]. CTA: presenter delivers CTA straight to camera; caption repeats it.

**Script notes:** write it as one great answer to an unheard question. Start mid-thought: "So here's what nobody talks about..." Conversational contractions throughout.

**Watch-outs:** the presenter is a brand presenter, never a named real person; some platforms require AI-content disclosure.

## 4. Viral hook stack

**Aesthetic:** three rapid pattern interrupts in the first 5 seconds, then a single payoff. This is a structure, not a look; visuals are standard UGC.

**Treatment:** Setting: [everyday location], changes slightly on each hook cut. Camera: handheld selfie framing with a hard cut on every hook. Lighting: natural. Actions: presenter delivers hook 1, hard cut, hook 2 restated sharper, hard cut, hook 3 as a challenge, then settles into the demo with the product. Pacing: 3 cuts in the first 5 seconds, then steady. Product moments: product enters at the settle, label readable, in hand at CTA. CTA: direct, single action.

**Script notes:** the three hooks attack the same problem from three framings (use the hook framework groups from the shhots-ugc-ads skill if installed: one problem-aware, one solution-aware, one unaware). Payoff must pay off the strongest hook.

**Watch-outs:** three weak hooks are worse than one strong one; cut the stack to two if the third is filler.

## 5. Talking kids

**Aesthetic:** a child presenter reviewing a family product with unfiltered honesty; bright, safe, daylight settings.

**Treatment:** Setting: bright family kitchen or living room, daytime. Camera: stable framing at the child's eye level, slight distance. Lighting: soft daylight, high-key, nothing moody. Actions: child presenter examines the product with genuine curiosity, reacts honestly, demonstrates the simplest use. Pacing: unhurried; let reactions breathe. Product moments: product central and accurate throughout. CTA: adult voiceover delivers the CTA over the closing shot.

**Script notes:** short simple sentences a child would actually say; honesty is the charm, so include one imperfect observation. Only user-supplied claims.

**Watch-outs (mandatory):** never for age-restricted products, supplements, medicines, or health products. The user must check their ad platform's current policies on depicting minors and AI-generated content before running the ad.

## 6. Talking animals

**Aesthetic:** a pet with subtly animated speech delivering the pitch deadpan; the comedy is the matter-of-factness.

**Treatment:** Setting: [home environment matching the pet: couch, kitchen floor, garden]. Camera: locked-off at pet eye level, punch-in on punchlines. Lighting: natural, warm. Actions: the animal sits with the product beside it and "speaks" with subtle mouth animation, glancing between camera and product; one physical interaction with the product (paw, nose). Pacing: deadpan pauses; the silence sells the joke. Product moments: product beside the animal throughout, label readable, nudged forward at CTA. CTA: the animal delivers the CTA flatly.

**Script notes:** write the animal as unimpressed but convinced. First person. The best line is the understated one.

**Watch-outs:** anthropomorphized opinions are fine; product claims still come only from the allowed list.

## 7. Whiteboard explainer

**Aesthetic:** a hand drawing the story on a whiteboard in fast motion, markers squeaking, diagrams building as the voiceover explains.

**Treatment:** Setting: clean white surface filling the frame. Camera: top-down or straight-on, following the drawing hand, zooming out as the board fills. Lighting: even and shadowless. Actions: a hand sketches [problem diagram], crosses it out, sketches [solution flow] step by step; the real product image appears pinned or inset at the solution step, rendered accurately. Pacing: drawing speed matched to voiceover beats. Product moments: product inset at the moment the explanation resolves, again at CTA. CTA: the hand writes the CTA and underlines it.

**Script notes:** structure as problem, why it happens, how [product] addresses it (allowed claims only), CTA. Number the steps out loud.

**Watch-outs:** never diagram a mechanism the allowed claims do not state.

## 8. Stop motion

**Aesthetic:** real-object animation: the product and props move in discrete steps, arrangements assemble themselves, tactile and playful.

**Treatment:** Setting: clean tabletop with [material: paper, fabric, wood] props in brand colors. Camera: locked-off top-down and 45-degree alternating. Lighting: soft directional daylight, consistent across frames. Actions: props slide, stack, and orbit around the stationary accurate product in stepped motion; packaging opens itself; elements assemble into a final composition. Pacing: rhythmic steps, synced to a simple percussive beat. Product moments: the product is the still center the whole time. CTA: props spell or frame the CTA in the final arrangement.

**Script notes:** minimal or no voiceover; on-screen text carries any claim. Sound design does the charm.

**Watch-outs:** motion consistency matters more than complexity; fewer props moving cleanly beats a busy frame.

## 9. Animated human body (supplements and wellness education)

**Aesthetic:** stylized, non-graphic anatomy illustration: a translucent human silhouette with highlighted systems, clean medical-explainer look.

**Treatment:** Setting: neutral gradient background with a stylized translucent human silhouette. Camera: slow push toward the highlighted region, then back out. Lighting: soft clinical glow on the highlighted system. Actions: the silhouette highlights [the body system named in the user's approved claim]; simple particle or pathway animation illustrates only what the approved claim states; the real product appears beside the silhouette, accurately rendered. Pacing: calm, instructional. Product moments: product on screen whenever a claim is spoken. CTA: product centered with CTA text; include any disclaimer text the user supplies.

**Script notes:** every line that touches the body must map one-to-one to an approved claim the user supplied in writing. Neutral verbs ("supports", only if supplied). No cure, treatment, or prevention language unless the user explicitly supplied it as approved.

**Watch-outs (mandatory):** suggest the user confirm the regulatory requirements for supplement advertising in each market they run ads in, including required disclaimers; do not provide legal advice. Stylized illustration only, nothing graphic.

## 10. Toy brick motion

**Aesthetic:** a miniature world built from generic construction bricks, assembling in stepped stop-motion; bright plastic colors, studded surfaces.

**Treatment:** Setting: a miniature scene built from generic plastic construction bricks in [brand-adjacent colors], no recognizable branded toy elements. Camera: tabletop level with slow lateral moves, macro on assembly moments. Lighting: bright, toy-commercial clean. Actions: brick structures assemble themselves in stepped motion to build [context: a shelf, a scene, a stadium] around the real product; brick figures react to it. Pacing: brisk assembly beats with one reveal pause. Product moments: the accurate product is the finale the bricks build toward. CTA: bricks assemble into the CTA text.

**Script notes:** playful narrator voiceover; build-up structure mirrors the physical assembly.

**Watch-outs (mandatory):** generic bricks only. Never depict LEGO logos, branding, minifigure likenesses, or trade dress in the treatment or script; trademark exposure lands on the advertiser.
